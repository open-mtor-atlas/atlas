Age-associated decline of Lamtor5 drives immunosenescence and systemic aging via cGAS-mediated paracrine inflammation.

Lv N, Tang Y, Zhang W, Zhang S, Zhang M · 2026 · Cell Death and Differentiation · Atlas ID LV2026

Age-driven loss of Lamtor5, a key lysosomal mTOR activation complex subunit, drives macrophage immunosenescence and systemic aging phenotypes in mice by unleashing cGAS-STING paracrine inflammation; Lamtor5 restoration in aged mice reverses these phenotypes.

Abstract

Lamtor5 (late endosomal/lysosomal adapter, MAPK and mTOR activator 5) was identified as an age-dependent factor controlling innate immune cell function. Age-associated Lamtor5 decline impairs mTOR-dependent macrophage signaling, triggering cGAS-mediated paracrine inflammatory senescence. Restoring Lamtor5 in aged mice rescued immunosenescence and attenuated systemic aging phenotypes.
