mTOR Academy · Beyond the Atlas

Free courses and lectures

The Academy teaches one thing, the mTOR pathway, from the studies in this Atlas. The free courses and talks below cover the ground around it: the cell biology it assumes, the signalling above it and the biology of ageing it feeds into. None of them is ours. Each one is here because it teaches something a lesson in the Academy leans on.

Where to start

New to biology

Four steps, about a week of evenings before the course itself.

  1. Michael Hall's talk, The Story of TOR (25 min)
  2. David Sabatini's three-minute video
  3. Two MIT lectures on cell signalling (about 100 min)
  4. Lesson 01 here in the Academy

Already studying biology

Read and watch these next to the lessons they match.

  1. Sabatini's NIH lecture, with lesson 02
  2. The LibreTexts chapter, next to lessons 02, 06 and 07
  3. The de Duve Lectures by Ballabio and Sabatini, with lesson 05
  4. Yale's Understanding Medical Research, before lesson 10

How to read the cards

Beginner secondary-school biology is enough   Student written for university biology students   Research graduate level, built around reading papers  
how much of it is about mTOR: three bars means all of it, one bar means useful background
My pick the five I would start with if you only have time for a few: one for each kind of need, from a first talk to judging a study. The rest are here for when one of them leaves you wanting more.

Every course here can be followed for free. On edX (MITx), Coursera and FutureLearn the free track usually comes without a certificate: a verified certificate costs money, and on some courses the free access ends after a set time, so it pays to go through them in one run.

Michael Hall · iBiology

The Story of TOR (Target of Rapamycin)

Michael Hall found TOR in yeast in 1991. He tells the story from a soil sample taken on Easter Island to the two protein complexes that control how big cells grow. English subtitles, and the video can be downloaded.

The lysosome and autophagy
