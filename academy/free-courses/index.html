mTOR Academy

Free courses and lectures

The Academy teaches one thing, the mTOR pathway, from the studies in this Atlas. The free courses and talks below cover the ground around it: the cell biology it assumes, the signalling above it and the biology of ageing it feeds into. None of them is ours. Each one is here because it teaches something a lesson in the Academy leans on.

Links checked 28 September 2026

There is no big online course about mTOR alone. mTOR turns up as a chapter inside courses on signalling, metabolism and ageing, so every entry says how much of it you will actually get.

How to read the cards

  • Beginnersecondary-school biology is enough
  • Studentwritten for university biology students
  • Researchgraduate level, built around reading papers

Every course here can be followed for free. On edX (MITx), Coursera and FutureLearn the free track usually comes without a certificate: a verified certificate costs money, and on some courses the free access ends after a set time, so it pays to go through them in one run.

About mTOR itself

Lecture · Michael Hall · iBiology

The Story of TOR (Target of Rapamycin)

Michael Hall found TOR in yeast in 1991. He tells the story from a soil sample taken on Easter Island to the two protein complexes that control how big cells grow. English subtitles, and the video can be downloaded.

Textbook chapter · LibreTexts · Fundamentals of Biochemistry

mTOR and Nutrient Signaling

A free university textbook chapter: the two complexes, how rapamycin binds, how leucine reaches mTORC1 through sestrin and GATOR, and how energy (AMPK) and insulin (Akt and TSC2) feed in. Reads well next to lessons 02, 06 and 07.

The biology the lessons assume

Course · MIT · MIT Learn (edX)

MITx 7.00x Introduction to Biology: The Secret of Life

MIT's introductory biology course, taught by Eric Lander and colleagues. It needs only secondary-school biology and chemistry and covers every term on the What you need first page, and a lot more.

Course · KAIST · Coursera

Basic Principles of Cell Signaling

How a signal travels from a receptor into the cell. The second week covers receptor tyrosine kinases and insulin signalling, the route that reaches mTORC1 through Akt, and a later week covers how cells sense oxygen and energy. mTOR is not named in the syllabus, but this is the wiring lesson 07 builds on.

An MIT seminar built on reading research papers about how tumour cells change their metabolism, the Warburg effect among them. mTOR is not in the syllabus by name. It suits someone who has finished the Academy and wants practice with primary literature.

Ageing and nutrient sensing

Course · University of Groningen · FutureLearn

Why Do We Age? The Molecular Mechanisms of Ageing

Telomeres, DNA damage, epigenetics and stem cells, written for a general audience. Week five covers calorie restriction and lifespan, the nutrient-sensing side of ageing where mTOR sits.

Course · Buck Institute · Buck Academy

Introduction to the Biology of Aging

Written for non-scientists by researchers at the Buck Institute. It explains the idea that ageing links diseases as different as cancer, Alzheimer's disease and diabetes. The course page does not mention mTOR by name.

Where to start

  • New to biology: Michael Hall's talk and David Sabatini's three-minute video, then MITx 7.00x or the KAIST signalling course, then lesson 01 here.
  • Already studying biology: The LibreTexts chapter next to lessons 02, 06 and 07, then Sabatini's de Duve Lecture with lesson 05, and MITx 7.06.1x if you want the cell biology in depth.
