Statement 1 of 4 · AKT-TSC · wording 863a9a38

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Akt/PKB inhibits TSC1/TSC2

Akt phosphorylates TSC2, disabling the complex. Growth-factor signalling works by removing a brake, not by pressing an accelerator.

Limits it states

In mouse liver, Akt phosphorylation of TSC2 is the main route by which insulin switches on mTORC1, but feeding activates hepatic mTORC1 without it; that response needs dietary protein instead (KAL2026). The biochemical mechanism itself was shown in cultured human cells (INO2002).

settled in the literature · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Animal model

Supporting

  • INO2002 Inoki K et al., Nature Cell Biology 2002
  • KAL2026 Kalafut KC et al., Nature Communications 2026 your paper
  • LAP2026 Lapp SC et al., JCI insight 2026 your paper
  • MAN2002 Manning BD et al., Molecular Cell 2002 your paper
  • POT2002 Potter CJ et al., Nature Cell Biology 2002

Our specific doubt

Is the boundary right: insulin uses this step in mouse liver, feeding does not (KAL2026)? And is “disabling the complex” the right verb, or does MEN2014 make “moving TSC off the lysosome” the better description of what Akt phosphorylation does?

Statement 3 of 4 · STRESS-TSC · wording 92ea3ada

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Energy & cellular stress recruits TSC1/TSC2

Many different stresses do the same physical thing: they drag TSC2 onto the lysosome, right where Rheb is. That relocation, not a change in TSC2 amount, is the switch.

Limits it states

Mammalian cell studies (DEM2016, MEN2014). Relocation of TSC2 to the lysosome is shown in cultured cells under several stresses; not tested in tissue or in people.

emerging, few independent studies · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Supporting

  • DEM2016 Demetriades C et al., Nature communications 2016
  • MEN2014 Menon S et al., Cell 2014 your paper

Our specific doubt

We say many stresses do the same physical thing and that relocation, not TSC2 amount, is the switch (DEM2016, MEN2014). Is that too general?

Statement 7 of 8 · MTORC1-SREBP · wording 78208c51

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

mTORC1 activates SREBP1 / SREBP2

mTORC1 drives the SREBP transcription factors, which turn on the genes for making fat and cholesterol - growth needs membrane, not just protein.

Limits it states

Mammalian cell studies (POR2008, PET2011) and an emerging rather than settled consensus. Cultured cells; the size of the effect in whole animals and in people is not measured by these studies. KID2013 adds primary mouse T cells: rapamycin blocked SREBP processing and nuclear entry after T-cell activation, and Scap-deleted T cells could not raise sterol synthesis. Still no human tissue data.

emerging, few independent studies · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Supporting

  • POR2008 Porstmann T et al., Cell metabolism 2008

  • KID2013 Kidani Y et al., Nature Immunology 2013

Our specific doubt

We label this “emerging”, not settled, despite DUV2010, POR2008, PET2011 and KID2013. Too cautious? And is “indirect” right?
