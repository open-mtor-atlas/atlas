Statement 1 of 4 · LYSO-MTORC1 · wording 8734f28b

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Lysosome required-for mTORC1

In the canonical nutrient-dependent route, mTORC1 has to reach the lysosomal surface to be switched on, because that is where Rheb is waiting. SAN2010 showed what happens when you take the first step away: anchoring mTORC1 to the lysosome permanently makes the pathway amino-acid insensitive and independent of Rag and Ragulator, but still dependent on Rheb. Recruitment and activation are two steps, and that experiment separates them. The requirement is stated for this route, not for every pool of mTOR in the cell.

Limits it states

Scoped to amino-acid-driven activation. Live BRET imaging in this corpus (BOU2020) resolves mTOR activity in the cytosol, at the lysosome, in the nucleus and near mitochondria, so mTOR signalling is measurably not one pool. This map draws the lysosomal route and keeps the other localisations declared as open rather than asserting there are none. FER2024 refines this further: lysosomal mTORC1 (fed by local lysosomal proteolysis) and cytoplasmic mTORC1 (fed by exogenous amino acids) phosphorylate distinct downstream substrates — the lysosome is required for one regulatory sub-pool of mTORC1, not for all of it.

settled in the literature · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Supporting

  • SAN2010 Sancak Y et al., Cell 2010
  • ZON2011 Zoncu R et al., Science 2011
  • FER2024 Fernandes SA et al., Nature Cell Biology 2024
  • KOR2011 Korolchuk VI et al., Nature cell biology 2011 your paper
  • CUI2025 Cui Z et al., Nature 2025

My question

Your KOR2011 supports this. I limited the requirement to amino-acid-driven activation and noted that other pools exist (BOU2020, FER2024). Does lysosome positioning fit under “required for”, or is it a separate statement the Atlas is missing?

Statement 2 of 4 · STRESS-TSC · wording 92ea3ada

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Energy & cellular stress recruits TSC1/TSC2

Many different stresses do the same physical thing: they drag TSC2 onto the lysosome, right where Rheb is. That relocation, not a change in TSC2 amount, is the switch.

Limits it states

Mammalian cell studies (DEM2016, MEN2014). Relocation of TSC2 to the lysosome is shown in cultured cells under several stresses; not tested in tissue or in people.

Statement 3 of 4 · MTORC1-SENESCENCE · wording c37f6a1d

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

mTORC1 activates Senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP)

mTORC1 sustains the inflammatory secretory output of cells that are already senescent, by promoting IL1A translation. Inhibiting it quietens that output without reversing the proliferative arrest.

Limits it states

Three studies, all in cells or a rat disease model. LAB2015 and NAR2011 measure the secretory output of cells that are already senescent (IL1A translation; IL-6/IL-8 production fuelled by mTOR at the TASCC). CHEN2026C (human disc cells, rat needle-puncture model) shows mTORC1 hyperactivation after TSC2 loss together with senescence and SASP, both blocked by rapamycin - a disease-model result, not a general demonstration that mTORC1 drives cells into senescence. That is why this arrow lands on the secretory programme and not on senescence itself.

emerging, few independent studies · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Animal model

Supporting

  • LAB2015 Laberge RM et al., Nature Cell Biology 2015
  • CHEN2026C Chen Xianglong et al., Advanced Science 2026
  • NAR2011 Narita Masako et al., Science 2011
  • WAN2017 Wang Rong et al., Aging Cell 2017

My question

I limited this to the secretory output of cells that are already senescent. Your CAR2017 shows mTORC1 stays on in senescent cells and that they depend on it. Does it support this statement, or should it be a separate one?

Statement 4 of 4 · RAPA-LONGEVITY · wording 66b18861

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Rapamycin activates Longevity

Rapamycin extends lifespan in genetically heterogeneous mice even when started late in life - one of the most influential results in the geroscience arm of this Atlas.

Limits it states

The lifespan result is mouse only. No human lifespan or healthspan trial is held in this corpus and EVERLAST has no results yet; the closest human survival data here, STE2026, is observational, at immunosuppressive doses in kidney transplant recipients, and found no benefit, which is why it sits on this edge as a conflicting study rather than as support. BIT2016 shows a non-continuous schedule can still extend post-treatment life expectancy, but the result is dose- and route-specific: in the 8 mg/kg/day intraperitoneal arm males gained +60% post-treatment life expectancy (p=0.02) while females showed no survival benefit (p=0.261) and a shift toward aggressive haematopoietic cancers (16/16 vs 6/12, p=0.002); in the same paper's 126 ppm dietary encapsulated-rapamycin arm survival rose significantly in BOTH sexes, with a Cox model finding no evidence that sex modified the treatment effect (p=0.904). Cite the arm, not just the paper. ARR2016: an intermittent regimen (2 mg/kg every 5 days) started as late as 20 months of age extended lifespan in female C57BL/6J mice; the abstract reports the effect in females only.

settled in the literature · human relevance: not tested in people · strongest evidence: Animal model

Supporting

  • BIT2016 Bitto A et al., eLife 2016
  • KHA2014 Khapre RV et al., Aging (Albany NY) 2014

  • ARR2016 Arriola Apelo SI et al., The journals of gerontology. Series A, Biological sciences and medical sciences 2016
  • ANI2011 Anisimov VN et al., Cell Cycle 2011
