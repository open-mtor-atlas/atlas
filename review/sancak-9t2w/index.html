Statement 1 of 4 · RAGULATOR-RAG · wording 5a60ea62

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Ragulator activates Rag GTPases

Ragulator does two jobs on the Rags. It tethers the Rag heterodimer to the lysosomal surface, and it acts as an unusual exchange factor: first reported as a GEF for RagA/B (BAR2012), its exchange activity was later placed on RagC, where releasing GTP unlocks the inactive state (SHE2018B). Loading RagA with GTP is done by arginine-bound SLC38A9 (see SLC38A9-RAG).

Limits it states

Human cells, Drosophila and purified recombinant proteins (BAR2012, SAN2010, SHE2018B). The exchange activity was first assigned to RagA/B and later to RagC, so the exact step is still being refined. Whole-animal and human relevance are not tested here.

settled in the literature · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Supporting

  • BAR2012 Bar-Peled L et al., Cell 2012
  • SAN2010 Sancak Y et al., Cell 2010 your paper
  • SHE2018B Shen K et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America 2018

Statement 4 of 4 · GLN-RAG · wording 26e55c78

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Glutamine activates Rag GTPases

Glutamine burned through glutaminolysis raises alpha-ketoglutarate, which promotes RagB GTP loading and mTORC1 translocation to the lysosome. This edge asserts the Rag-dependent route only.

Statement 5 of 8 · RAG-MTORC1 · wording cb15e5cc

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Rag GTPases recruits mTORC1

The active Rag dimer grabs Raptor and physically drags mTORC1 to the lysosomal surface. This is a relocation, not an activation - the kinase is moved to where its activator waits.

Limits it states

Rag paralogues are not interchangeable: RagC/D sets substrate specificity downstream (RagD favors TFEB/TFE3 phosphorylation via tighter LAMTOR binding, while both Rags are involved for non-lysosomal substrates like S6K), and RagA/B sets the response to amino acid withdrawal (RagB-expressing cells keep mTORC1 lysosomal and active even when starved) — GOL2022.

settled in the literature · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Animal model

Statement 6 of 8 · LYSO-MTORC1 · wording 8734f28b

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Lysosome required-for mTORC1

In the canonical nutrient-dependent route, mTORC1 has to reach the lysosomal surface to be switched on, because that is where Rheb is waiting. SAN2010 showed what happens when you take the first step away: anchoring mTORC1 to the lysosome permanently makes the pathway amino-acid insensitive and independent of Rag and Ragulator, but still dependent on Rheb. Recruitment and activation are two steps, and that experiment separates them. The requirement is stated for this route, not for every pool of mTOR in the cell.

Limits it states

Scoped to amino-acid-driven activation. Live BRET imaging in this corpus (BOU2020) resolves mTOR activity in the cytosol, at the lysosome, in the nucleus and near mitochondria, so mTOR signalling is measurably not one pool. This map draws the lysosomal route and keeps the other localisations declared as open rather than asserting there are none. FER2024 refines this further: lysosomal mTORC1 (fed by local lysosomal proteolysis) and cytoplasmic mTORC1 (fed by exogenous amino acids) phosphorylate distinct downstream substrates — the lysosome is required for one regulatory sub-pool of mTORC1, not for all of it.

settled in the literature · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Supporting

  • SAN2010 Sancak Y et al., Cell 2010 your paper
  • ZON2011 Zoncu R et al., Science 2011 your paper
  • FER2024 Fernandes SA et al., Nature Cell Biology 2024

Statement 7 of 8 · VATPASE-RAGULATOR · wording d8824b1f

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

v-ATPase required-for Ragulator

The lysosome's proton pump talks to Ragulator from the inside out: amino acids inside the lysosome change v-ATPase, which changes Ragulator, which changes the Rags.

Limits it states

The 'inside-out' model requires amino acids to accumulate inside the lysosome first; how they get there for every amino acid is not fully mapped.

emerging, few independent studies · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure
