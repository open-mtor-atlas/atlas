I am trying to make sure I understand how folliculin and the Rag GTPases control mTORC1 at the lysosome. Two of these four statements rest on your papers; one asks which protein is the GAP for RagC/D, and one lists studies that seem to point the other way. Did I get them right?

Statement 1 of 4 · FLCN-RAG · wording 66b18c8b

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

FLCN / FNIP1/2 activates Rag GTPases

FLCN-FNIP is the GAP for RagC/D: it makes RagC/D hydrolyse its bound GTP, which puts that half of the Rag dimer into the GDP-bound state. 'Activates' on this edge means exactly that: FLCN-FNIP sets the nucleotide state of RagC/D. It does not switch on every Rag. The dimer can hold mTORC1 only when both halves are in the right state (RagA/B GTP-bound and RagC/D GDP-bound), and other factors act on the RagA/B half. Its direct effect is SUBSTRATE-SPECIFIC: it gates TFEB and TFE3 phosphorylation, while canonical outputs like S6K1 and 4E-BP1 largely carry on without it in the short term.

Limits it states

FLCN's direct effect is substrate-specific: losing it blocks mTORC1 phosphorylation of TFEB/TFE3 while leaving S6K1 and 4E-BP1 largely intact in the short term (NAP2020). That is not the whole of Birt-Hogg-Dube, though. Constitutively active TFEB/TFE3 drive a feedback loop that hyperactivates mTORC1, and in kidney and lung that mTORC1 hyperactivation is itself a key driver of cyst and tumour formation (Di Malta 2023, EMBO Mol Med, PMID 36987696; Ferniani 2026, FER2026 in corpus; Cao 2026, JCI, PMID 41697734, 3 BHD patient lungs + mouse). So the regulation is tissue- and time-dependent, not a flat absence of hyperactivation.

settled in the literature · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Supporting

  • LAW2019 Lawrence RE et al., Science 2019 your paper
  • SHE2019 Shen K et al., Cell 2019
  • NAP2020 Napolitano G et al., Nature 2020

Our specific doubt

FLCN-FNIP is a GAP that puts RagC/D into the GDP state. Is “activates Rag GTPases” the right headline for that? And is the limit fair: the direct effect is on TFEB/TFE3, yet FLCN loss can still overactivate mTORC1 in kidney and lung through TFEB/TFE3 feedback?

Statement 2 of 4 · LARS-RAG · wording c249b357

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

LARS (leucyl-tRNA synthetase) activates Rag GTPases

In this model LARS acts as a GAP for RagD, doing for the RagC/D half what FLCN does - an alternative route from leucine to the Rags.

Limits it states

Human cell biochemistry only, and cell-type dependence was never mapped. The reaction it claims - GAP activity on the RagC/D half - is the same reaction this Atlas assigns to FLCN, and the two assignments have never been reconciled head-to-head. Read together with LEU-LARS: if the upstream leucine-sensing claim does not hold, this edge loses its input.

disputed: at least one study points the other way · human relevance: not tested in people · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Supporting

  • HAN2012 Han JM et al., Cell 2012

Our specific doubt

We file LARS as a RagD GAP (HAN2012) under a field dispute with FLCN that was never tested head to head. Is that fair, or has the field settled it?

Statement 3 of 4 · RAG-MTORC1 · wording cb15e5cc

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

Rag GTPases recruits mTORC1

The active Rag dimer grabs Raptor and physically drags mTORC1 to the lysosomal surface. This is a relocation, not an activation - the kinase is moved to where its activator waits.

Limits it states

Rag paralogues are not interchangeable: RagC/D sets substrate specificity downstream (RagD favors TFEB/TFE3 phosphorylation via tighter LAMTOR binding, while both Rags are involved for non-lysosomal substrates like S6K), and RagA/B sets the response to amino acid withdrawal (RagB-expressing cells keep mTORC1 lysosomal and active even when starved) — GOL2022.

Statement 4 of 4 · MTORC1-TFEB · wording 71d1c473

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

mTORC1 inhibits TFEB

Active mTORC1 phosphorylates TFEB right on the lysosomal surface, trapping it in the cytoplasm. Switch mTORC1 off and TFEB walks into the nucleus.

Limits it states

RagD specifically (more than RagC) promotes TFEB/TFE3 phosphorylation via tighter LAMTOR binding — GOL2022 shows this is paralogue-specific, not a generic Rag-dimer property.

settled in the literature · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Statement 5 of 8 · GATOR1-RAG · wording cb8a611a

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

GATOR1 inhibits Rag GTPases

GATOR1 acts as a GAP: it forces RagA/B to hydrolyse GTP, flipping the Rag heterodimer into the inactive shape that cannot hold mTORC1.

Limits it states

Human cell biochemistry with cancer genomics (BAR2013) and a cryo-EM structure (SHE2018). GAP activity is shown on purified and cellular Rags; the cited studies do not test it in whole animals or in people.

Statement 6 of 8 · SLC38A9-RAG · wording 8b82dad4

The statement, as it appears in the Atlas

SLC38A9 activates Rag GTPases

SLC38A9 sits in the lysosomal membrane and reads arginine on the inside. Arginine-bound SLC38A9 acts as an exchange factor that loads RagA with GTP, switching the Rag dimer on (SHE2018B); it also pumps essential amino acids, including leucine, back out into the cytosol.

Limits it states

Mammalian cell studies and purified-protein work (REB2015, WAN2015, JUN2015, WYA2017, SHE2018B). Cell and biochemical evidence; whole-animal and human relevance are not tested here.

settled in the literature · human relevance: plausible in people, not shown directly · strongest evidence: Molecular — cells, biochemistry, structure

Supporting
