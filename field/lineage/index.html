Lab lineage

Labs pass on more than results. A PhD student or postdoc learns a way of asking questions, takes it to their own lab and turns it in a new direction. This map follows 116 such links: 133 scientists in the tree, who trained where, what they worked on there and what their own lab studies now. Another 24 mentors, whose labs people passed through before or after their main one, are named next to that person.

  • Alejo Efeyan Postdoc, 2008-2015 Immunity Made RagA-GTP knock-in mice and showed that Rag-dependent mTORC1 control is essential for autophagy and survival right after birth. Also trained with Manuel Serrano (PhD)

  • Gregory A. Wyant PhD, PhD 2019 (MIT Biology) Metabolism Showed that SLC38A9 senses arginine and also exports essential amino acids such as leucine from lysosomes; co-developed rapid lysosome purification (LysoIP). Also trained with William G. Kaelin Jr. (Postdoc)

  • Robert A. Saxton PhD, PhD 2018 (MIT Biology; co-advised with Thomas U. Schwartz) Immunity Solved the crystal structures of Sestrin2 with leucine and CASTOR1 with arginine, showing how amino acids are recognized upstream of mTORC1. Also trained with K. Christopher Garcia (Postdoc)

How this map is made

This is an editorial reconstruction, not a citation analysis. A link appears only when two independent sources agree: a PubMed paper that the trainee wrote with the mentor while working at the mentor's institution, and a separate page that names the mentor, such as a lab alumni list, a CV, a thesis record or an official profile. Links with one source so far are kept back until a second one turns up.

Many scientists trained in more than one lab. The map draws each person under one mentor (the one whose work appears most often in the Atlas) and shows the other links as dashed lines and in the side panel. Positions and dates come from those sources and can lag behind job moves. If you spot a mistake, use the report button at the bottom of the page.
