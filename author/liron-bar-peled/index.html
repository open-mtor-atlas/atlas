Liron Bar-Peled

Defined the GAP and GEF switches that control the Rag GTPases · PhD, Sabatini lab, Whitehead Institute/MIT · Rullo Family Endowed Chair in Cancer Research, Massachusetts General Hospital & Harvard Medical School

As a graduate student in David Sabatini's lab, Liron Bar-Peled showed that the Ragulator complex acts as a GEF (guanine nucleotide exchange factor) that switches the Rag GTPases on, and then identified GATOR1 as the GAP (GTPase-activating protein) complex that switches them back off — together defining the molecular on/off switch that gates amino-acid signalling to mTORC1.

Bar-Peled now leads his own lab at Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical School, where his research has broadened to how cancer cells manage metabolic and oxidative stress, including the discovery that SLC38A9 senses lysosomal arginine to activate mTORC1.
