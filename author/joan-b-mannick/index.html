Joan B. Mannick

Ran the first clinical trials showing mTOR inhibition improves immune function in older adults · MD, Harvard Medical School · led Novartis's clinical mTOR-inhibitor aging program · co-founder, resTORbio · SVP & Chief Medical Officer, Altos Labs

Portrait: Altos Labs

Joan Mannick trained in internal medicine at Harvard before moving into drug development, first at Genzyme and then, from 2010, in a newly formed aging-focused unit at Novartis. There she led the first randomized clinical trials testing whether low-dose mTOR inhibition could improve immune function in elderly volunteers, rather than just extending lifespan in animal models.

Her trials showed that low-dose rapalogs improved influenza-vaccine responses and reduced the rate of infections in people over 65 — among the first direct evidence in humans that partially inhibiting mTOR could counter an aspect of immune aging. She co-founded resTORbio, a Novartis spinout, to develop RTB101 along these lines, and has since led drug development at Life Biosciences and Tornado Therapeutics before becoming Chief Medical Officer at Altos Labs in 2025.
