Richard A. Miller

Co-directs the Interventions Testing Program that showed rapamycin extends lifespan · MD, PhD, Yale · Professor of Pathology · Director, Paul F. Glenn Center for Biology of Aging Research, University of Michigan

Richard Miller has spent his career developing rigorous methods to test whether interventions actually slow aging in mice, rather than merely treating a single disease. He directs one of the three collaborating sites of the NIA's Interventions Testing Program (ITP), which he helped design specifically to guard against the false positives that had plagued earlier longevity research.

Through the ITP, Miller's group made the seminal contribution to the 2009 discovery that rapamycin extends mouse lifespan by more than 20%, even when treatment begins in middle age — a result that reshaped the aging field's view of mTOR as a druggable target for healthy aging. His lab continues to study the genetics and cell biology of long-lived mouse strains.
