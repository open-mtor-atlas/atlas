Oliver's mTOR Atlas · Researchers · Sue Menon

Sue Menon

Showed insulin controls mTORC1 by moving the TSC complex on and off the lysosome · PhD, Yale University (2008) · postdoctoral fellow, Manning Lab, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health (2008–2013) · now Research Investigator, Novartis

As a postdoc in Brendan Manning's lab, Sue Menon showed that insulin triggers the TSC complex to acutely dissociate from the lysosomal surface — where a pool of Rheb and mTORC1 resides — through Akt-mediated phosphorylation of TSC2, physically separating the brake (TSC) from the switch (Rheb) it otherwise keeps off. It gave a spatial explanation for how growth-factor and nutrient signals converge on the same lysosomal on/off switch without simply overriding each other.

Her other work in the Manning lab traced mTORC1's downstream reach into disease biology — including how chronic mTORC1 activation alone is sufficient to cause liver cancer in a mouse model, and how mTORC1 drives lipogenesis through SREBP1c — before she moved into industry research at Novartis.
