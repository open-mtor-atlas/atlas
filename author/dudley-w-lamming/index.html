Dudley W. Lamming

Showed rapamycin's side effects come from hitting mTORC2, not mTORC1 · Postdoc, Whitehead Institute/MIT (Sabatini lab) · Professor of Medicine, University of Wisconsin–Madison · Co-director, Wisconsin Nathan Shock Center

Portrait: UW–Madison Dept. of Medicine

During postdoctoral training in David Sabatini's lab, Dudley Lamming discovered that many of rapamycin's unwanted metabolic side effects, such as insulin resistance and elevated blood glucose, come from off-target inhibition of mTORC2 rather than from blocking mTORC1, the complex thought to mediate the drug's lifespan benefit. This distinction reframed how the field thinks about designing safer mTOR-targeted longevity interventions.

Since 2014, Lamming has run his own lab at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where he studies how nutrient-responsive pathways, especially dietary protein and branched-chain amino acids acting through mTOR, can be tuned to promote healthy lifespan without rapamycin's side effects. He served as president of the American Aging Association in 2023–2024.
