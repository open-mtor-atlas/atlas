Ioannis P. Androulakis

Models how circadian rhythm and nutrient-sensing pathways like mTORC1 stay synchronized · PhD, Chemical Engineering, Purdue University (1993) · Professor, Biomedical Engineering & Chemical and Biochemical Engineering, Rutgers University · adjunct, Department of Surgery, Robert Wood Johnson Medical School

Portrait: Rutgers University, Dept. of Biomedical Engineering

Ioannis Androulakis runs a systems-biology and -pharmacology group at Rutgers focused on the interactions between circadian rhythms, inflammation and metabolism. In LU2026, his group built a mathematical model of the liver's peripheral clock in which mTORC1 and GCN2-ISR signalling jointly determine how well the clock stays synchronized to feeding rhythms under nutrient stress — a computational companion to the experimental case, elsewhere in this Atlas, that mTORC1's pattern over time, not just its average level, is what matters.
