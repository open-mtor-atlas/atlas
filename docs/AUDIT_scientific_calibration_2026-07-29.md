## 4. Additional overstatements discovered

You flagged two. The sweep found **twenty-eight more**. Four of these I only caught after writing the validator and running it against the corpus — which is the argument for having the validator rather than a one-off read-through.

**Absolute language where the design didn't support it (6)** — `MCC2011` "the first to prove", `FRA2013` "the cleanest human proof", `GUE2006` "proved" + "definitive", `JOH2013` "the definitive Nature review", `KIM2003` "proved", `HAR2014` "proof", `FLY2013` "Striking proof", `HSI2012` "therapeutic proof-of-concept", `THO2012` "to settle a long debate".

**Uncontrolled trials presented without their design caveat (2)** — `KRU2010` (open-label, n=28) and `IYE2012` (n-of-1 exceptional responder, "pinpoints"). `WAG2014` was a third: another single-patient case stated as an established explanation.

**Observational studies framed causally (3)** — `BAN2014` (n=180,926 retrospective cohort, no confounding caveat), `MAR2026` (retrospective multicentre cohort, "supporting selective use" with no selection-bias note), `IYE2012`.

**Mechanistic or animal work described in clinical terms (4)** — `ZHU2026` and `GAO2026` both "establishing/identifying … a therapeutic target" from mouse and in-vitro models; `BO2026` "demonstrated" from docking plus one cell line; `HSI2012` "therapeutic proof-of-concept" from mouse models.

**Causal claims with no species or system scope (5)** — `LV2026` ("causes … systemic aging"), `LAM2012`, `BEN2008`, `ROD2011`, `VAL2017`. In each case the model organism was recorded in the metadata but absent from the sentence, so the claim read as universal when quoted out of the table.

**Graph and prose layer (5)** — the Tuberous sclerosis entity description ("the clearest proof"), the TSC→mTORC1 mechanism edge ("the clearest human proof"), the rapamycin→mTORC2 edge context ("intermittent dosing largely avoids this", unscoped and without noting the absence of human data), the H2 knowledge-gap basis, and the lineage open-question node.

### What I deliberately did not change

Calibration cuts both ways, and over-hedging is its own failure mode:

- `MAN2021` — "a promising biomarker is not a proven benefit". Correct use of a strong word, in the negative, about a failed phase 3.
- `HAN2025` — "human evidence does NOT yet prove rapamycin delays aging". Correctly strict.
- `MAR2026` — "no OS benefit demonstrated". Negated; accurate.
- `KAH2000` — "the trial that established rapamycin as an immunosuppressant". Historical fact about a phase III with n=719 followed by approval, not an evidence claim.
- `HEI1991` — "targets whose disruption causes rapamycin's toxicity". Yeast genetics; genuinely causal in that system, which is named.
- `INO2003`, `SAN2010`, `SET2011`, `FEL2009` — "Established the energy-sensing arm", "Established WHERE mTORC1 gets switched on", etc. These describe what the paper established *in the field's history*, not a claim about clinical causality.

### The two-axis question, resolved explicitly

Your instruction was: *"Every eligible peer-reviewed **primary** study receives an A–D evidence tier."* That sentence is now the Atlas's claim, and it is true. But it needed one more line to be complete, because the Atlas grades on **two different axes** that disagree about narrative reviews by design:

| | Narrative review | Preprint | Registered trial |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Pyramid_Level` (study **type**) | outside the 1–5 pyramid | outside | outside |
| `Evidence_Tier` (evidence **strength**) | **D** | none — "Preprint" | none — "Registered trial" |

Tier D is defined in Airtable as "mechanistic / in vitro / theoretical / **narrative review**" — so a review carries the weakest grade rather than no grade. That is coherent, and I did not silently redefine it. What was missing was any statement of it on the page, which is why "every study is graded" and "outside the evidence hierarchy" could both appear without either being wrong.

I added a short **"How this relates to the A–D tier"** paragraph directly beneath the "Outside the evidence hierarchy" section stating all of it plainly: primary studies get A–D; narrative reviews get D but sit outside the pyramid; **preprints and registered trials get no A–D tier in either direction**, because grading them would invent a strength that does not exist.

Your core instruction is satisfied and enforced: no preprint carries an A–D tier, and R4 blocks the deploy if one ever does.

## 5. Validation rules

New file: **`validate_claims.py`**, wired into both `deploy.bat` and `deploy.sh` as a blocking gate (`--strict`) that runs before anything is committed or pushed.

| Rule | Flags | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** uncontrolled-as-confirmation | Phase I / first-in-human / open-label / single-arm / dose-escalation design, with no randomised or placebo marker, paired with *confirms / demonstrates / avoids / causes* | ERROR |
| **R2** observational-as-causal | Retrospective, cohort, case-control, cross-sectional or registry design paired with a causal verb; also warns when such a study carries no confounding or association caveat | ERROR / WARN |
| **R3** mechanistic-as-clinical | Tier C or D record using clinical language ("in patients", "therapeutic target", "clinical evidence") without a "no human data" scope; **R3b** flags causal verbs in non-human studies with no species named in the sentence | WARN |
| **R4** tier-on-out-of-hierarchy | A–D tier on a record whose `Pyramid_Level` is Preprint or Registered Trial, or whose `Peer_Reviewed` is No | ERROR |
| **R5** absolute-language | *proves, proven, proof, definitive, conclusive, unequivocal, beyond doubt, settles, establishes that, direct confirmation, guarantees* anywhere in study findings, gap bases, relation mechanisms, entity descriptions or hand-written page prose | ERROR (data) / WARN (prose) |
| **R6** tier-scope-overclaim | Copy that reasserts blanket A–D grading for every study | ERROR |

Two design choices worth knowing about:

**Negation is exempt.** "not proven", "no OS benefit demonstrated", "does not yet prove" are the *cautious* uses. A linter that flagged them would push the Atlas toward hedging its own null results — the opposite of the goal. `negated()` checks a 70-character window either side.

**Term-of-art exemptions.** "all-cause mortality", "the underlying cause", "root cause" are not causal verbs and are excluded.

## 6. Confirmation

Every claim in the Atlas has been checked against the design of the study it rests on, in the corpus data, the pre-rendered pages, the knowledge-gap layer, the entity graph, the mechanism edges, the hand-written homepage and About copy, the meta descriptions and JSON-LD, and the Airtable source records. `validate_claims.py --strict` returns **0 errors and 0 warnings**.

Two honest qualifications on that confirmation:

1. **The validator checks calibration, not truth.** It knows the pairing of (claim strength, study design). It cannot tell you whether a finding is correctly summarised from its paper — that still needs a human reading the source.
2. **It is a lexical tool.** It catches the vocabulary of overstatement. A sentence that overstates through implication or selective omission, using only mild verbs, would pass. R2's missing-caveat warning is the partial defence against that, but it is not complete.

Within those limits: no scientific claim in the Atlas now asserts more than its cited evidence supports, and the deploy gate will block the next one that tries to.
